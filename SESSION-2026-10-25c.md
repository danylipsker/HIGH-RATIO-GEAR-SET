# Session 2026-10-25 (c): mates for an imported assembly

GEARS GENERATOR now has the set as a family card (its engine builds the six gears, the housing
and the bearings from `gearset.py`'s geometry and writes one STEP). SolidWorks opens that STEP
as an assembly of fixed components with generic names, so `GearBuilder.cs` gained
`MateImportedAssembly(IModelDoc2 asmDoc, GearModel g)`:

- tells the six gears apart by their bounding boxes against the layout (sun: shaft from -2b to
  beyond section B; planet about +a1; thin ring about -e over both sections; carrier; fixed ring
  in section A; output ring in section B, or the output cup to the end of its shaft with a
  housing), each name to its best unused component, rejected when off by more than 2 mm + 10 %;
- keeps the fixed ring fixed, frees the other five, and adds the same mates as `Build`
  (`AddMates`): Front Planes coincident with the fixed ring's, concentric per moving part, gear
  mates to the sun with ks, kp, kt, ko;
- reports every step in `BuildResult.MateReport` and the component boxes in `Log`.

The stubs (`solidworks/stubs/sldworks.cs`) learnt `GetComponents`, `UnfixComponent`,
`Component2.GetBox` and `IsFixed`; `compile_check.sh` passes. The copy in GEARS GENERATOR
(`src/GearGen.SolidWorksAddin/HighRatio/GearBuilder.cs`) is this file unchanged; its
`SwAddin.InsertCore` calls the method after the STEP import. The standalone add-in's own build
is unchanged (it mates the parts it makes, as before).

## Motion Study

`GearBuilder.AddMotionStudy` (after the mates, both in `Build` and in `MateImportedAssembly`):
`IModelDocExtension.GetMotionStudyManager` → `CreateMotionStudy` named "Sun drive", `Activate`,
`SetDuration(10 s)`, the sun's input stub face (the one the mates use) pre-selected with mark 0,
`CreateMotor(swRotaryMotor, swMotorConstantSpeed, 60 rpm)`: 10 sun turns, 7.2° on the output ring
of the 1:500 set. The swmotionstudy interop is driven late-bound through `dynamic` (the add-in
references no further assembly; `compile_check.sh` now adds Microsoft.CSharp); the enum values are
read from the installed `SolidWorks.Interop.swmotionstudy.dll` (next to SLDWORKS.exe, in
api\redist, or the GAC), else the documented 1 / 1. `BuildResult.MotionStudy` carries the line
the message boxes show; every failure is reported in `MateReport` and the log, never thrown.
Flags: `GearBuilder.AddMotor`, `MotorRpm`, `MotorSeconds`.

## The dialog, in the user's order

`GearSetForm.cs` rebuilt around the way Dany works: **1 module, 2 gear ratio, 3 Find solutions**
on one line; **4 the table** of every tooth set that gives the ratio (best first, now with a
Sense column and the overall length); **5 the picked solution** -- the title names the row,
the dimensions, the folder name and Build follow it. The search range (sun and planet ranges,
forced counts), the design rules (ring tooth difference, max working pressure angle, max ring
OD), face width / backlash / root fillet, an exact tooth set and the presets are under an
"Advanced" check box, folded.

Found on the way: with the default rules this mechanism's smallest ratio is about **1:216**
(sun up to 30); 1:200 needs a 35-tooth sun (35/10/91/99/135/132), 1:100 is out of reach even
with a 60-tooth sun. The old dialog answered 1:100 with "nearest" rows 428 % off; the new one
lists nearest rows only within 15 % and otherwise says what the mechanism reaches and that a
planetary or compound planetary set is the right choice below about 1:200.
